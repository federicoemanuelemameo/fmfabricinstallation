Feel the Space

Feel the Space turns a textile into an interface. A spatially intelligent installation for premium retail, it lets a Nishijin-ori fabric sense a visitor's movement, gaze and touch - and lets the space answer through light, motion and the fabric itself, without a single screen.

In premium retail, the most valued moments are the ones a screen gets in the way of: approaching a product, looking at it, touching it. Feel the Space keeps those gestures exactly as they are and makes the material itself the medium of the conversation.

Situated conceptually in Hosoo's flagship store in Kyoto, the installation is conceived as a multi-panel of Nishijin-ori fabric, mounted on a motorised rail and lit by projection. A depth camera reads the visitor's position and gaze; conductive fabric layered behind the textile reads pressure. Everything the visitor does is read through the space, and everything the space says is said through the fabric.

Three distances, one material. The fabric responds differently as the visitor moves closer, from a passing glance to the hand on the weave.

01 — Approach

At rest, the fabric is still. A faint light traces its natural pattern, enough to suggest that it is alive.

When a visitor passes by, a soft pulse of light reveals the weave and follows them, reading their position, distance and speed. It draws attention without ever demanding it.

02 — Gaze

When the visitor stops and looks, the system tracks which part of the fabric holds their focus. That look is the trigger: animated patterns bloom across the textile and the panel glides along its rail, turning itself toward the person.

The fabric is no longer a surface being looked at. It looks back.

03 — Touch

Up close, the Nishijin-ori fabric becomes the interface itself. Capacitive sensing through the weave reads the pressure of the hand, and the visuals projected onto the textile respond to it: the harder and longer the touch, the faster and denser the pattern moves.

Touch is the most intimate step and the most important gesture in retail. Here it surfaces the craft held within the fabric: centuries of textile-making, revealed through the hand.

What the fabric shows

What appears on the textile is the textile's own story. Nishijin-ori is made through eight specialised steps, from design and colour to thread, warp and loom, before a single line is woven. The projections reinterpret each step as a living pattern, in the same visual language across the whole installation.

Built in WebGL and driven by the touch sensing in the fabric, the eight frames move as the hand moves: the pressure of the visitor sets the speed and density of the composition, so every touch reveals the craft a little differently.

For a textile brand, the fabric is the product, the story and the store. Feel the Space lets it be the interface too.

01 — The fabric tells its own story

Heritage is a brand's strongest asset and the hardest to show at the point of sale. Here the craft is not on a placard or a screen: it appears on the weave itself, the moment a visitor touches it. Eight steps, or your own.

02 — More touch, longer stays

In premium retail, touch is the gesture that precedes purchase. The installation is built to invite it: light draws the visitor in, the panel turns toward them, and the fabric rewards the hand. Every stage extends the time spent with the material.

03 — A flagship moment, without screens

A living, kinetic textile is a reason to visit the store and to talk about it afterwards. It gives the space a signature no digital display can, and it stays true to the material, because the material is the medium.

Under the fabric

A depth camera and a computer-vision pipeline handle person detection, distance, velocity and gaze. Conductive fabric behind the Nishijin-ori panel handles touch. A lead-screw actuator moves the panel along its rail; a projector draws on it. None of it is visible: the only thing the visitor meets is the weave.

Bringing it to your textile

Feel the Space was developed with Nishijin-ori in Kyoto, but nothing in it is tied to one fabric. The sensing layer sits behind any woven panel; the projected visuals are built from the brand's own production steps; panel scale, rail length and position follow the store.

Adapting it means three conversations: your fabric, your craft story, your store.
